Model assumptions⌄

Fare eligibility. A free ride is not fare evasion. SFMTA's free-fare programs include youth 18 and under, eligible low-income seniors, eligible people with disabilities, and Access Pass riders. Low-income adults are not automatically exempt: Clipper START offers eligible adults a 50% discount, while some senior and disability fares are discounted. Valid passes and transfer rides also have no new fare due. The model excludes no-new-fare trips from added fare revenue and citations, and prices discounted trips at half fare.

Unknown rider shares. The default 20% no-new-fare share and 10% discounted-fare share are adjustable placeholders, not measured Muni trip shares. SFMTA's FY25–26 budget reports $23.4M in fare-program impact, but that dollar figure does not tell us what portion of trips fall into each category. These sliders let users see how that uncertainty changes results.

Inspection and behavior. Seeing an inspection adds +40 pp to the cohort's next-ride chance to pay; a personal check adds another +8 pp. From a 50% starting point, that gives 90% after seeing and 98% after being checked, before spillover. Effects fade toward baseline with the selected half-life (30 days by default). The chance-to-pay curve describes riders with a fare due; it does not imply that eligible free riders should pay.

Operations. Inspectors work an 8-hour shift (11 AM–7 PM) and complete two 5-minute checks per ride when schedules permit. The 4-checks-per-minute rate is an editable sandbox assumption. Journeys follow scheduled weekday GTFS stop times, not real-time vehicle locations; this representative timetable repeats across the 90-day simulation. Bus loads, stop-by-time boardings, rider behavior, visibility, and inspection outcomes are synthetic. “Best modeled return” ranks trips by expected fare recovery plus collected citations and avoids recently used routes. Route variation adds unpredictability. Fine amount is $134; the 5% citation rate and 50% collection rate are editable assumptions, not measured Muni outcomes.
